Page 1: Researching the Brief

Exploring the general area, three possible topics, existing solutions, and final project idea.

Part A: Researching the Brief

General Area of Analysis

My general area of analysis is Health & Lifestyle. Specifically, how everyday habits such as sleep, diet, physical activity, and mental wellbeing interact and affect overall health outcomes.

Why This Area Appeals to Me

Health and lifestyle is an area that affects everyone directly, making findings immediately relatable and meaningful. I am personally interested in health & fitness. I have been wondering how small daily habits (such as how much we sleep) can have a compounding effect on my health over time.

Three Possible Topics

Topic 1 (Chosen)

Sleep Quality & Mental Health

Topic: The relationship between sleep patterns and mental health indicators such as anxiety, depression and happiness.

Interest: I haven't been getting a lot of sleep recently and I want to find out if it'll have a big impact on my mental health and happiness.

Purpose: To determine whether sleep duration is a reliable predictor of happiness and mental health outcomes.

Topic 2

Diet, Exercise & BMI

Topic: How diet quality and physical activity levels correlate with weight status.

Interest: I am curious whether the phrase "You can't out train a bad diet, but you can outdiet bad training" is accurate.

Purpose: To identify which factor (diet or exercise) shows a stronger association with healthy weight outcomes.

Topic 3

Lifestyle & Diabetes Risk

Topic: Which lifestyle indicators are most associated with diabetes or pre-diabetes diagnoses.

Interest: I have a lot of extended family that have type 2 diabetes and I am curious about what habits may have caused it.

Purpose: To identify the strongest lifestyle predictors of diabetes risk.

Citations: Kaggle -Mental Health and Lifestyle Habits 2019–2024 (Atharva Soundankar) · CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System · Diabetes Health Indicators Dataset (UCI ML Repository)

Part B: Existing Solutions

1. PMC Meta-Analysis - Sleep Quality & Mental Health (1998–2024)
PurposeTo evaluate the overall effect of improving sleep quality on mental health outcomes including depression, anxiety, and stress in adults.
Data54 papers involving 10,196 adults (1998-2024). Data came from validated questionnaire scores measuring sleep quality alongside depression, anxiety, and stress levels.
ConclusionImproving sleep quality significantly reduced depression and anxiety, though no significant difference was found in stress levels compared to standard care.
2. National Sleep Foundation - Sleep in America Poll (2023)
PurposeTo investigate the association between sleep duration and satisfaction with depressive symptoms in the general US adult population.
DataA national probability-based sample collecting self-reported sleep duration, satisfaction, and PHQ-9 depression scale scores.
ConclusionPeople who slept the recommended 7-9 hours were nearly 50% more likely to be free from elevated depressive symptoms. Those dissatisfied with sleep were nearly eight times as likely to experience moderate-to-severe depression.
3. SleepFoundation.org - Sleep Quality & Mental Health Survey (2024)
PurposeTo explore how self-rated mental health relates to sleep quantity and quality in everyday adults.
DataA survey of 1,000 US adults (April 2024) collecting self-rated mental health, nightly sleep hours, and subjective sleep quality ratings.
ConclusionPeople with below-average mental health slept nearly an hour less per night than those with above-average mental health and were three times as likely to rate their sleep quality as poor or very poor.

Part C: How Research Informed My Choice

Point 1

Because my research showed that sleep duration and subjective sleep quality scores are the most commonly measured variables in existing studies, I decided to focus my analysis on these same variables using the Kaggle dataset, as they are already included and well-documented.

Point 2

Because my research showed that happiness and mental health condition are the outcomes most consistently linked to sleep in large-scale studies, I decided to use happiness score and mental health condition as the key output variables in my analysis.

Point 3

Because my research showed that existing studies tend to use large clinical trial datasets, I decided that using a publicly available Kaggle lifestyle dataset would offer a different, more accessible angle, allowing me to explore the same relationships in everyday, non-clinical survey data.

Part D: Final Idea

Final Project Idea: I will analyse a Kaggle dataset on mental health and lifestyle habits to investigate whether sleep duration and stress level are associated with happiness score and mental health condition across 3,000 participants from seven countries.
Limitation: The dataset relies entirely on self-reported data, which may be inaccurate as participants may over- or under-estimate their sleep hours, stress levels, and mental health scores.
